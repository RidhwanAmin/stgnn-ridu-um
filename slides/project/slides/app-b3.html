Appendix B3: Three-Stage Estimation and the Retention Correction

Stage 1 · Fit retention, then freeze

Silent loss only: pulse deficit by coupling ratio q (Zheng et al., 2008) × motion from Pulsewatch (lag-corrected ECG R-peaks). Fitted outside the network.

Stage 2 · Train and weight

Encoder sees intervals and the quality flag; p̂ calibrated on validation subjects; each reported beat weighted by 1/π̂PAC(m, q).

Stage 3 · Report or abstain

Coverage gate, a fixed reporting rule: a day is reported only if every 6-hour block reaches θblock.

Held for questions. Three stages. Stage 1 fits retention for silent loss only and freezes it: pulse retention by coupling ratio, using the published threshold of about 55% of the sinus RR (Zheng et al., 2008), times motion retention estimated on Pulsewatch with per-session lag correction. The product is an assumption, because Pulsewatch R-peaks carry no beat types, and no retention value is claimed: the 8-subject check found matching falling with motion but detector-dependent. Stage 2 trains the encoder with the quality flag as input, calibrates beat probabilities on validation subjects, and weights each reported beat by the inverse of its retention. Stage 3 applies the coverage gate, a fixed rule rather than a learned layer. The table is the key answer to double counting: the Garmin keeps abnormal beats and flags them, so they stay in the input unweighted; a marked gap reduces observed time; only silent loss is reweighted. Because RO4a's pulse-deficit degradation uses the same threshold, RO4a is reported across the full severity sweep. Pending supervisor reconfirmation (E1).
