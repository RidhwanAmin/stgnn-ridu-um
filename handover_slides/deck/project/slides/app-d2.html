Appendix D2: From One Beat to an ESVEA Call

Primary ESVEA: 480 or more supraventricular beats per day (Haeusler et al., 2025); secondary: 30 or more per hour (Binici et al., 2010), equal to 720 per day; runs reported separately. Scaling analysable hours to a day is biased low, most on active days and in high-burden patients (§2.5), so Equation 3.2 divides each 6-hour block by its own observed hours.

Hourly shape from the block ratios of Larsen et al. (2016): 0.87, 0.63, 1.11, 1.40. The usable strip is illustrative of night-weighted analysable time (Merschel & Reinhardt, 2022).

Held for questions. Three steps from one beat to a decision. The unit is one premature atrial beat: early, then a pause. The window is 24 hours, about 100,000 beats; PACs are not spread evenly, they are lowest around 6 a.m. and highest in the afternoon and evening. The threshold is 480 or more supraventricular beats per day, the MonDAFIS criterion; 30 per hour, Binici, is secondary and equals 720 per day. The strip under the bars is the point of the whole estimator: the watch is usable mostly at night, when PACs are fewest, so multiplying what it sees up to 24 hours under-counts. That is why each 6-hour block is divided by its own observed hours. The hourly shape and the strip are illustrative, not study data.
