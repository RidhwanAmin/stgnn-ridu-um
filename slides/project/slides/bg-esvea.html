Background: Atrial Ectopy and the Monitoring Decision

Excessive supraventricular ectopic activity (ESVEA) is an atrium producing too many premature beats. It is usually symptomless, but it marks a remodelled atrium.

ESVEA after acute ischaemic stroke (≥48 h Holter: portable heart-rhythm recorder)

3.3%

AF (atrial fibrillation) detected on the same Holter

18.5% vs 5.9%

New AF by 24 months, with vs without ESVEA (Haeusler et al., 2025)

  • Defined as ≥30 supraventricular ectopic complexes per hour or any run of ≥20 (Binici et al., 2010); stroke cohorts also use ≥480 supraventricular beats per day or runs of 10 to 29 s (Haeusler et al., 2025).
  • Predicts AF after ischaemic stroke, pooled HR (hazard ratio) 2.91 across 20 studies (Yang et al., 2022), and stroke beyond AF, HR 1.96 when censored at AF (Larsen et al., 2015).
  • Finding AF changes treatment: anticoagulation lowers ischaemic stroke in device-detected AF (RR, relative risk, 0.68) at the cost of more major bleeding (RR 1.62) (McIntyre et al., 2024); the absolute benefit is larger after prior stroke or TIA (mini-stroke) (Shoamanesh et al., 2025).

Ectopy is therefore measurable in far more patients than AF, and before AF is documented.
