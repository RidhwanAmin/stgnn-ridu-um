Background: Monitoring Capacity and the Smartwatch

Prolonged monitoring finds more AF but is a limited resource, so it must be allocated.

  • Duration drives yield: AF in 5.1% with ≤72 h, 15% with ≥7 days and 29.15% with 3 months (Dussault et al., 2015); implantable monitors reach 34% beyond 24 months (Tsivgoulis et al., 2019).
  • Ectopy as selector: 90-day AF probability rises from <9% with <100 atrial premature beats per 24 h to 40% beyond 1,500 (Gladstone et al., 2015); pooled OR 4.16 across 12 stroke studies (Tao et al., 2021).
  • Clinical scores as the alternative: no monitoring needed, but AUROC 0.54 to 0.94 across cohorts (Kishore et al., 2022), and no model excellent on external validation (Helbitz et al., 2025).
  • One ECG day is not enough: first-day sensitivity for ESVEA was 44.6% against 14-day telemetry (Måneheim et al., 2023).
  • The smartwatch: AF detection pooled sensitivity 95%, specificity 97% across 26 studies (Barrera et al., 2025), but coverage is partial: worn on 21.2 of 30 days after stroke (Ding et al., 2023); 22.3% of PPG minutes unusable vs 1.3% of ECG (Gruwez et al., 2021).

A smartwatch worn after discharge could observe ectopy repeatedly, without consuming Holter capacity.
