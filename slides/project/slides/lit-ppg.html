Common thread: beat intervals carry ectopy, but wearable evidence stops at window classification, and analysable time is night-weighted while PACs peak by day. PPG burden has been validated against ECG for AF only (Zuo et al., 2026); no study reports a smartwatch-derived daily PAC count.

About 1.5 minutes. Three points. First, beat intervals carry ectopy: PACs are the main source of false AF alarms. Second, the best wearable work classifies 30-second windows with PAC and PVC merged; Vargova and colleagues (CinC 2026, accepted abstract) show beat-level PAC versus PVC from intervals alone is possible, trained on simulated PPG, which is the closest comparator to our design. Third, the bottom two rows together: analysable smartwatch time is concentrated at night, PACs in the afternoon. That pairing is the informative-missingness argument. If asked about Han: the 106 subjects are 72 post-stroke participants over 14 days plus 34 from an AF trial.
