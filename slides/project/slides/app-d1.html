Appendix D1: What Is Counted: a PAC, Motion Artefact or AF

Counted

Wrist still. One early, smaller pulse, then a longer pause. The device may flag it as abnormally short. The model scores it as p̂ᵢ.

Not a PAC

Wrist moving. Beats are missed or split and flagged as motion-affected. Flag and motion are inputs; silent loss is corrected by 1/π̂(m); a marked gap leaves observed time.

A different finding

Every interval differs, with no steady pace, for minutes or more. In the pilot, AF vs PAC/PVC windows: AUROC 0.968 to 1.000 (Table 4.1).

Schematic waveforms, not device data. Open circle above a beat: device low-confidence flag. Flag semantics: §3.4 (Garmin, 2023); low-confidence beats at low motion are candidate rhythm events.

Held for questions, for panel members outside cardiology. Three things make beat intervals irregular and only one is counted. A PAC is one early, weaker beat followed by a longer pause, with the wrist still. Motion artefact happens while the wrist moves: the watch misses a beat, giving one long interval with no short one before it, or picks up a false peak; the Garmin flags these, and the accelerometer tells the model the wrist was moving. AF is irregular throughout, with no interval repeating; it is the arrhythmia that monitoring after stroke looks for, not an ectopic count. The flag is binary and marks both motion-affected beats and intervals judged abnormally short or long, so a PAC tends to be flagged too: that is why the flag is an input, not a deletion rule.
