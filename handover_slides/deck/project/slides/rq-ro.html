Aim, Research Questions and Objectives

Aim: to establish whether smartwatch beat intervals (time between heartbeats), analysed with an estimator that models signal quality and unobserved time, can yield a daily atrial ectopy estimate whose error is characterised against ECG labels under simulated smartwatch losses and whose coverage after stroke is adequate, as a step towards comparison with ECG-derived ESVEA thresholds.

RQ1: How much ectopy information is lost when a PPG (optical wrist pulse) waveform is reduced to a quality-filtered beat-interval series?

RO1: To investigate the information lost when a PPG waveform is reduced to a filtered beat-interval series, separating the loss from absent pulse morphology from the loss due to beat rejection.

RQ2: Can a two-scale beat-interval ST-GNN (graph-based deep learning model) that uses per-beat quality flags (low-confidence beat markers) as input estimate atrial ectopy rate more accurately than sequence-based baselines?

RO2: To develop and evaluate a two-scale beat-interval ST-GNN (beat-graph within windows; epoch-graph across the day) that estimates atrial ectopy rate from interval sequences and their quality flags.

RQ3: Can a 7-day smartwatch protocol in post-stroke survivors yield enough analysable and stable data to compute a per-day ESVEA classification?

RO3: To evaluate the deployability of a 7-day smartwatch protocol in post-stroke survivors by analysing data yield, threshold computability and day-to-day stability against pre-specified progression criteria.

RQ4: Does substituting a smartwatch-derived ectopy estimate for the ECG-derived count change ESVEA classification, and how do wearable-based selections compare with clinical risk scores?

RO4: To evaluate and validate the substitution of a smartwatch-derived ectopy estimate for the ECG-derived count: (a) ESVEA reclassification (change in ESVEA status) under simulated device losses, and (b) agreement with score-based selections.
