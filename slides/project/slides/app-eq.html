Appendix: Retention-Weighted Daily Rate (Equations 3.1 to 3.4)

(3.1)   π̂k(m, q) = π̂pulse(k, q) · π̂motion(m)

(3.2)   R̂(d) = Σh (Lh/24) · [ Σi∈A(d,h) p̂i / π̂PAC(mi, qi) ] / τ(d,h)

(3.3)   report R̂(d) only if c(d, h) ≥ θblock for every block h

(3.4)   R̄ = Σh (Lh/24) · [ Σd∈V Σi∈A(d,h) p̂i / π̂PAC(mi, qi) ] / Σd∈V τ(d,h)

  • A(d,h): beats the device reports in day d, block h; p̂i: calibrated PAC probability; mi: motion; qi: coupling interval ÷ preceding interval; τ(d,h): observed hours. Gaps go to τ; flagged beats stay as input, unweighted
  • Blocks h: 00–06, 06–12, 12–18, 18–24 h (Lh = 6 h); θblock: per-block minimum fixed after device verification; V: valid days
  • Per-day values (3.2, 3.3) for stability; ESVEA from the pooled participant count 24 · R̄ (3.4); sensitivity: shrinkage towards Larsen et al. (2016), λ = 1 h (0.5 to 4 h)
  • Retention corrects only silent loss: pulse deficit by coupling interval (Zheng et al., 2008; π̂pulse(other, q) = 1) × motion from Pulsewatch (ECG R-peaks, PPG, accelerometer magnitude, per-session lag); product is an assumption; frozen
  • 8-subject check: matching above chance at high motion and falling with motion, but retention depended on the detector (0.850 vs 0.752) → detector sensitivity analysis; primary detector: Elgendi et al. (2013) algorithm on all segments, authors' peaks as alternative

If asked how the quality flag is used rather than deleted: the Garmin keeps abnormally short or long beats with a low-confidence flag and marks a gap where it gives no beat (Garmin, 2023). Gaps enter the observed time; flagged beats stay in the model's input and are not reweighted, so no beat is corrected twice. Only beats lost silently, such as a premature beat with no wrist pulse, are corrected: each reported beat is weighted by the inverse of its retention probability, as in inverse-probability weighting, and the rate is normalised by observed time per block. Beat probabilities are temperature-scaled on validation subjects before they are summed, because a sum of uncalibrated probabilities inflates the count. Retention multiplies a pulse part, by coupling interval, by a motion part from Pulsewatch; the product is an assumption because Pulsewatch R-peaks carry no beat types. The released alignment is not beat-accurate, so a lag is fitted per session; a lag method that does not need labelled segments is an RO2 task. The 8-subject check did not pass its locked rule: matching was above chance at high motion and fell with motion, but absolute retention differed by detector, so no retention value is claimed. Each block is weighted by its length, with no external profile. The gate refuses to report a daily count unless every 6-hour block meets the minimum; ESVEA status uses Equation 3.4, which pools each block across the participant's valid days, so a block thin on one day is informed by the same block on other days. A calibrated simulation showed pooling as accurate as shrinkage towards the published profile, and better than averaging only fully covered days when coverage is poor; a day can still count as valid wear for the RO3 feasibility outcome under the day and night rule.
